## Scope, applicability and limitations

Theorem A states that fewer than 2^k clauses guarantee satisfiability.
The proof samples a uniform assignment and bounds the expected number
of false clauses by m/2^k. Its per-clause calculation requires k distinct
variables, exactly the proper-clause convention in PROBLEM.md. This is not
a guarantee for clauses of at most k literals or repeated literals.

The following paragraph gives sharpness at n=k by taking all 2^k sign
patterns. P002 gives the equivalent counting proof, the explicit lower
bound for the number of satisfying assignments, and the extension to
n>k and m>2^k under our unused-variable and clause-repetition conventions.
Only this elementary result is used; broader complexity remarks in the
teaching notes are not adopted. No novelty or independent review is claimed.
