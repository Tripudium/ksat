## Checks performed

1. **Model and tail event.** Proper k-clauses use distinct variables; literal
   signs are independent and unbiased. A fixed assignment survives one clause
   with probability q=1-2^(-k), and m independent clauses with probability q^m.
   In the common clause process, T>m is exactly SAT at time m. The fixed-witness
   lower bound and union bound over 2^n assignments are valid without assuming
   independence between different assignments. The inequality includes m=0;
   its upper bound may exceed one, which is harmless.
2. **Almost-sure finiteness.** The upper tail tends to zero because 0<q<1.
   This also justifies treating T as an ordinary finite integer-valued random
   variable almost surely. No finite deterministic upper bound is assumed.
3. **Generating-function identity.** For z=e^theta>1 and every nonnegative
   integer t, z^t=1+(z-1) sum_{m=0}^{t-1} z^m. Tonelli applies to all terms and
   permits the expectation to be infinite. Inserting the tail bounds yields
   the two displayed bounds when zq<1. For zq=1 the lower sum has constant
   positive summands, so it diverges; for zq>1 its terms grow. For theta<=0,
   0<exp(theta T)<=1 proves finiteness, including theta=0. Thus the precise
   real domain theta<-log q is correct and the endpoint is correctly excluded.
4. **Coupon example.** When n=k=2, each clause type removes one distinct
   assignment, so UNSAT occurs precisely after all four types appear. The
   waiting-time convention is geometric on {1,2,...}, with means 1/p and
   variances (1-p)/p^2. At p=1,3/4,1/2,1/4 these give means 1,4/3,2,4 and
   variances 0,4/9,2,12. Consequently E T=25/3 and Var T=130/9>25/3. Future
   draws after each collection time are independent of the past, and the
   success probability depends only on how many types remain; this establishes
   independence of the waiting times, not merely their geometric marginals.
5. **Implication scope.** The example rules out the unmodified inequality
   Var(T)<=E T. It does not rule out a k-dependent variance constant. The finite
   positive mgf boundary rules out any finite all-real Poisson cgf upper bound
   for the centered variable because centering multiplies the mgf by a finite
   positive factor. It does not rule out a bound on a restricted tilt interval,
   a sub-gamma bound with a finite pole, or a different statistic.
6. **Comparison source.** The supplied `papers/sharp-phase.pdf`, Theorem 6.1,
   printed p. 216, explicitly states the rotation-volume variance bound
   Var(I)<=E I and the all-real Poisson-type centered cgf bound. The comparison
   in the example accurately identifies what fails to transfer verbatim.
