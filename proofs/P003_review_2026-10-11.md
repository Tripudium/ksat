## Coordinate-restriction Crofton identity

For each I of cardinality r, a coordinate face H_(I,b) meets S precisely when b belongs to the projection pi_I(S). There are exactly 2^r equally likely b, so the conditional probability is |pi_I(S)|/2^r. Uniformly averaging over the binomial(n,r) possible I gives the displayed normalization.

Each coordinate face of codimension r has one unique representation (I,b); thus the sampling scheme is indeed uniform over such faces. For r=0, the projection is a singleton empty tuple if S is nonempty and is empty if S is empty, giving exactly the asserted nonemptiness indicator. For r=n the projection is S itself, giving |S|/2^n. Empty S yields zero throughout. No independence, convexity, or nontrivial Crofton theorem is assumed: this is a finite counting identity.

## Ordered-distance moments and probability bounds

For any fixed vertex x, vertex transitivity gives Pr(x in gT)=|T|/2^n, hence the first moment. At distance d there are exactly 2^n*binomial(n,d) **ordered** pairs, including 2^n diagonal pairs when d=0. The signed coordinate permutation group is transitive on each such pair class: translate the first vertex to zero using coordinate reflections, permute the difference set to the target difference set, and translate zero to the target first vertex. The frozen proof states these operations in this order; a minor ambiguity in the earlier draft was corrected before freezing.

Consequently the inverse image under uniform g of any fixed ordered pair at distance d is uniform on that ordered-pair class. Its probability of lying in T squared is A_d(T)/(2^n*binomial(n,d)). Expanding Z squared over ordered pairs in S squared and collecting by d yields exactly the stated second moment. The d=0 term is |S||T|/2^n, equal to the first moment; no factor of two is missing.

Both moments are finite because 0<=Z<=2^n. When S and T are nonempty, their first moment is strictly positive, so the second is strictly positive and the Cauchy–Schwarz denominator is valid. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to Z times the indicator of Z>0 gives the lower bound. The upper bound uses the integer-valued property of Z, and is capped at one. If either set is empty, Z is identically zero, and the text explicitly avoids the undefined zero-over-zero expression.

Additional algebraic checks: singleton S,T yield first and second moments 2^(-n), with both probability bounds exact. Full S=Q_n yields E Z=|T| and E Z^2=|T|^2 because sum_d A_d(T)=|T|^2, so the lower bound correctly equals one for nonempty T. For the earlier S={000,110}, U={000,111} example, the second moments are 2/3 for S intersect gS and 1/2 for S intersect gU; these are consistent with the independently checked nonemptiness probabilities 5/12 and 1/2.
