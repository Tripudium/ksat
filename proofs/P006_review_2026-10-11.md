## Domain, normalization and stopped-path law

For any fixed nonempty S, a fixed surviving assignment satisfies each fresh clause with probability q. The union bound over S gives Pr(tau_S>m)<=|S|q^m, including m=0. This proves almost-sure absorption and finite exponential moments on the stated domain theta<a_k. The empty-state convention tau_empty=0 gives H=1 and both derivatives zero.

For every interior theta, there is a positive exponential margin epsilon with theta+epsilon<a_k. Polynomial factors in tau are bounded by a constant times exp(epsilon*tau), supplying an integrable dominator locally for all required derivatives. Thus the derivatives of log H are the mean and variance under the normalized exponential change of measure; finiteness is established before using them.

Conditioning on the first uniform clause gives H(S)=exp(theta) times the average of H(S_C). The displayed pi therefore sums to one and is positive. For every finite clause sequence first absorbed at t, multiplying its kernel probabilities telescopes exactly to exp(theta*t)/(N^t H(S)). The original probability of that sequence is N^(-t). Summing these probabilities over all finite absorbing sequences gives E exp(theta*tau_S)/H(S)=1. Hence the kernel has no missing mass on nonabsorbing paths. This handles state-preserving clauses and arbitrary repeats without assuming independence under the tilt. For an initially empty state, the zero-length stopped path has probability one and satisfies the same density convention.

The transformed process is Markov by its explicit state-dependent kernel. Conditional on a current state, its future stopped path is the same transformed continuation begun at that state. This justifies using the tilted means and variances after the first transition.

## Drift and variance occupation

The time from a nonempty state is one plus its continuation after the first clause. Total expectation gives the stated drift; total variance gives B_theta(S)+E v_theta(S_C). Immediate absorption contributes r=v=0 and causes no exception. The identities extend trivially to the empty absorbing state where needed in the finite iteration.

Iterating for a deterministic number ell of steps yields the finite sum and the remainder E v_theta(S_ell). For fixed n,k,theta there are finitely many subsets of Q_n, each with finite nonnegative variance. Their maximum is therefore finite, even though its size need not be useful computationally or uniform in n. Since v(empty)=0, the remainder is bounded by that maximum times Pr_theta(tau_S>ell), which tends to zero by the previously proved transformed absorption. Monotone convergence applies to the nonnegative B terms. No optional-stopping theorem or uniform-in-n bound is smuggled into this argument. At theta=0, H is identically one, pi is uniform, and the drift agrees precisely with S001's fresh-continuation identity.

## Conditional concentration calculation

Under (H+), the occupation identity bounds K'' on the nonnegative interval by V/(1-b theta)^3, where V=C_k n. Direct differentiation confirms that the second derivative of V theta^2/[2(1-b theta)] equals exactly V/(1-b theta)^3. Both functions have value and first derivative zero at zero. Twice integrating on a positive interval therefore gives the displayed cgf inequality with no missing factor.

For x>0, s=sqrt(2x/V) is positive and theta=s/(1+b s) lies strictly in (0,1/b), hence inside the mgf domain by the assumed 1/b<a_k. With t=V s+b V s^2/2, the Chernoff exponent is

    -[s/(1+bs)] [Vs+bVs^2/2] + Vs^2/[2(1+bs)] = -Vs^2/2 = -x.

Also t=sqrt(2Vx)+b x. Markov's inequality thus proves the stated upper-tail bound. If m is the displayed upward-rounded integer, {T_n>m} is contained in {T_n-mu_n>=t}, so Pr(T_n<=m)>=1-delta. This verifies the upper quantile assertion under the usual integer quantile convention.
