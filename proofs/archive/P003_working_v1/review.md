## Exact checks

1. **Cubical-complex definition and intersections.** If all vertices of a coordinate face lie in S, the same is true of all its subfaces. Thus the induced object is a cubical complex. Membership of a face in K(S intersect gT) is precisely simultaneous membership in K(S) and gK(T). Signed coordinate permutations preserve the coordinate-face collection.
2. **Finite group averaging.** There are `N_j=2^(n-j) binom(n,j)` coordinate j-faces, and signed coordinate permutations act transitively on them. The inverse image of a fixed face under a uniform group element is uniform on that orbit. Summation gives the stated product-over-N_j expectation; alternating a finite sum gives the Euler identity. Cases j=0, j=n and empty S or T satisfy the same proof. No independence of different faces is used or needed.
3. **SAT with zero Euler characteristic.** The two 3-clauses exclude exactly 000 and 111. They are nonadjacent, and their incident edge sets are disjoint, removing 6 of 12 cube edges; six edges remain. Each of the six square faces fixes one bit and contains precisely one excluded vertex, so all square faces and the full cube are absent. The remaining graph is the induced six-cycle. Therefore its Euler characteristic is zero despite six satisfying assignments.
4. **Expected face profile.** For a fixed j-face, the number R of selected variables among its fixed coordinates has the stated hypergeometric law. Given R=r, a clause fails at some vertex of the face if and only if all r fixed-coordinate literals are false; the distinct free variables can then be assigned to falsify the remaining literals simultaneously. The probability is exactly 2^(-r), including r=0. Clause independence justifies exponent m for m>=1. The separate m=0 case avoids any 0^0 convention and yields the full cube. Boundary checks: q_0=2^(-k), q_n=1; N_0=2^n, N_n=1; the expected vertex count agrees with the ordinary first moment.
5. **Failure of face-count log-concavity.** Among last-four-bit strings of weights 2, 3 and 4, there are 6+4+1=11 choices. Each permits exactly two first-two-bit choices with the required total even parity, giving 22 isolated vertices. Distinct even-parity vertices are never adjacent. Their last-four-bit weight at least two makes their distance from the stated square at least two, so none is adjacent to a square vertex. The complex is the disjoint union of a square and 22 points, with face vector (26,4,1,0,...). Thus 4^2<26*1. For the normalized sequence, N_0=64, N_1=192 and N_2=240; `(4/192)^2=1/2304 < 26/(64*240)`, so that claim also holds.
6. **Realizability as proper 6-CNF.** With n=k=6, each assignment has one unique clause containing each variable once that it falsifies. Conjoining the clauses excluding the complement of S deletes exactly those vertices and retains exactly S. This uses the project's proper-clause definition and introduces no relaxation.
7. **Face vectors do not determine intersection probability (`ex:face-profile-insufficient`).** The sets S={000,110} and U={000,111} each consist of two nonadjacent vertices, so both have face vector (2,0,0,0). Under the stated group, gS is uniform on the 12 unordered distance-two pairs. Exactly 3 such pairs contain either fixed vertex of S, and S itself is counted twice, giving 3+3-1=5 intersecting pairs and probability 5/12. The orbit of U consists of the four antipodal pairs; S meets two different such pairs, giving probability 1/2. This also follows by inclusion-exclusion: the expected intersection size is 1/2 in both cases, while the probability of a two-point intersection is 1/12 versus zero. The claimed obstruction to a formula based solely on the two face vectors is therefore valid. Excluding the six complement vertices with their corresponding proper 3-clauses realizes each set as asserted. No asserted discrete analogue of Alexandrov–Fenchel, Steiner positivity, or Brascamp–Lieb is required.
