## Model, integrability and mean bound

The sample space is precisely the project's independent proper k-clause
sequence. All probability calculations allow repeated clause types, while
individual clauses contain k distinct variables. Clause occurrences, including
equal types, are separate left vertices in the incidence graph.

A covering matching really does imply SAT: matched variables are distinct,
so setting each matched literal true creates no assignment conflict. Each
clause remains satisfied regardless of other literals. The augmenting-path
argument correctly produces a deficient clause set from a maximum matching.
In that argument, traversing unmatched edges from clauses and matched edges
from variables reaches all neighbors of every reached clause; a reached
clause's own matched neighbor was already reached. Thus the number of reached
variables is exactly one less than the number of reached clauses.

For 1<=t<=m<n, padding the deficient neighborhood to t-1 variables is valid.
The probability for fixed clause positions and this variable set is exactly
[binom(t-1,k)/binom(n,k)]^t, independently of signs. The stated union bound
therefore covers every matching failure, including repeated clauses. When
t-1<k the summand is zero, consistently with the subsequent upper estimate.

The ratio estimate holds factor by factor because, for 0<=j<k and t-1<=n,
(t-1-j)/(n-j)<=(t-1)/n. The identity
binom(n,t-1)=binom(n,t)t/(n-t+1), combined with the two standard binomial
upper bounds, yields exactly

    [t/(n-t+1)] [e^2 (m/n) (t/n)^(k-2)]^t.

Taking c=1/(4e^2), t<=m<=cn and k>=2 bounds this by
t*4^(-t)/[(1-c)n]. The infinite sum is 4/9. The constants D=4/[9(1-c)]
and d=c/2 are correctly stated, and c<1/4 gives D<16/27<1. The m=0 case
has an empty failure sum. Finally, n>=2, m+1>cn and T_n>=m+1 on T_n>m
give mu_n>cn(1-D/n)>=cn/2. Thus the claimed weak inequality mu_n>=dn
holds uniformly for every n>=k>=2, including n=k and small n.

P004's displayed exponential tail gives almost-sure finiteness and all finite
moments for each n. No uniform moment estimate beyond the stated mean bound
is silently used. The mgf is finite throughout the nonpositive intervals
appearing later.

## Rare early core and fixed negative tilts

There are N=B*binom(n,k) equally likely clause types. For one fixed k-element
variable set, the B! orders of all B sign patterns are distinct, disjoint
length-B prefixes. Their total probability is exactly B!/N^B. Every such
prefix is UNSAT. The elementary assignment-counting argument proves T_n>=B
for every clause sequence, so the event indeed forces equality T_n=B.
The proof only lower-bounds Pr(T_n=B); it does not claim to enumerate every
UNSAT cover of that size.

Using binom(n,k)<=n^k gives the stated logarithmic lower bound with
h_k=B log B-log(B!)>=0. Since the prefix probability is at most one,
log p_(n,k)=O_k(log n) in absolute value. For each fixed s>0, the upper and
lower uncentered mgf bounds are in the correct direction:

    -sB + log p_(n,k) <= L_n(-s) <= -sB.

They imply L_n(-s)/n -> 0. Adding s mu_n and applying the linear mean bound
gives liminf K_n(-s)/n>=sd, without any limiting threshold or convergence
assumption on mu_n/n.

For hypothetical finite C,b>0, the chosen
s=min{1/(2b),d/(2C)} is strictly within the allowed negative-tilt interval.
Since bs<=1/2, the target upper bound divided by n is at most
Cs^2<=ds/2, contradicting the lower limit sd>0. The proof therefore rules
out the target even if asserted only for all sufficiently large n. Its
additional restriction 1/b<a_k cannot help, since the contradiction uses
only negative tilts. All asymptotics keep k fixed.

## Shrinking tilt and tilted variance

For A>kB/d, delta=(dA-kB)/2 is positive. Substituting
s_n=A log(n)/n gives

    K_n(-s_n) >= (dA-kB)log n - AB log(n)/n - h_k.

The last two terms are dominated by delta log n for all sufficiently large
n, yielding the stated lower bound. Meanwhile s_n tends to zero, eventually
lies in every fixed interval (0,1/b), and the proposed upper bound is exactly
CA^2(log n)^2/[2n(1-bA log(n)/n)], which tends to zero.

Differentiation on a compact nonpositive interval is justified for fixed n
by the integrable dominator 1+T_n^2; the mgf is strictly positive there.
First and second tilted moments are continuous, so L_n'' equals the tilted
variance and is continuous up to zero. The endpoint derivatives may be
understood from the left; P004 also supplies a positive neighborhood of zero.
Centering gives K_n(0)=K_n'(0)=0. Applying Taylor's integral identity to
s -> K_n(-s) gives the displayed integral with kernel (s_n-u), with no sign
change missing. Its integral is s_n^2/2. Continuity on the compact interval
ensures a maximum, and substitution gives precisely

    Var_(-u_n)(T_n) >= (2 delta/A^2) n^2/log n.

This is an existence statement about an n-dependent negative tilt. It is
not an estimate of the untilted variance or a claim that the maximizing tilt
has been located.

## Scope, consequences and limitations

The final lower-tail claim uses n>=2B/d, which ensures B<=dn/2. On the core
event, T_n-mu_n<=B-dn<=-dn/2, so its probability lower bound is valid.

The compatibility sentence in the final remark describes what **this
obstruction alone does not exclude**. It does not prove an O(n) untilted
variance or a square-root central window; in particular it must not be read
as such an assertion for the separately understood k=2 control. For k>=3,
the proof leaves the cited variance manuscript claims untouched. Likewise,
no positive-tilt bound, exceptional-tail upper bound or conditioned-ensemble
transfer is proved here.

The result precisely refutes the proposed unconditional two-sided finite-tilt
target. It does not refute the general research programme, existing geometric
theorems, or an external variance theorem. No external proof, formal artifact,
novelty or exhaustive-literature claim was checked or established by this
review. Any substantive change to this frozen fragment reopens the affected
review scope.
