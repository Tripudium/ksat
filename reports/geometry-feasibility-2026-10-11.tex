\reportnote{Assessment}{%
  Useful finite-size SAT-probability diagrams are feasible on CPUs, starting
  with modest random 3-SAT instances. A bounded local pilot supports
  $n=50$--$200$ as a sensible initial range. On the mathematical side, exact
  discrete intersection identities are available, but they do not yet yield
  a transferred phase-transition theorem. The most promising analytic route
  is a finite-tilt variance argument for the clause hitting time. This note
  separates exact elementary results, proposed proof steps, measured costs,
  and conditional budget estimates.}

\section{Which parts of the geometric proofs can transfer?}
We retain the project model: $n\ge k\ge2$, $m$ independent clauses, exactly
$k$ distinct variables per clause, uniform variable sets and fair independent
signs; whole clauses may repeat. Write $S_m\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ for the
solution set, and $T_n=\min\{m:S_m=\varnothing\}$ in the common clause
sequence. The objective here is to find a useful discrete proof architecture
and assess experiments, not to replace this ensemble by a convex, planted,
or conditioned model.

\paragraph{The Euclidean integral-geometry paper.}
The proof in \citet[Sections 6--9]{lotz2026} has three separate ingredients:
an exact Steiner mixture identity, concentration of a positive
intrinsic-volume index, and a geometric identity that turns concentration
into a transition. If $L(\theta)=\log\mathbb E e^{\theta I}$ for the
rotation-volume index, Lemma 8.3 and Fact 8.4, pp.~225--227, give
\[
 L''(\theta)=\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_\theta}(J_\theta)
              -(n-L'(\theta))\le L'(\theta).
\]
Here $\mu_\theta$ has a log-concave density proportional to
$e^{-J_\theta}$, with a convex distance potential. Integrating this
\emph{tilted} variance bound gives Theorem 6.1's Poisson-type concentration.
The rigid-motion version uses a different potential and Nguyen's
inverse-Hessian variance inequality, Fact 9.5, p.~231. The generating-function
argument is reusable for discrete random variables; the convexity and
mixture identities furnishing its hypothesis require a new justification.

\paragraph{The conic paper.}
In \citet[Fact 5.7, Theorems 6.1 and 7.1]{almt2014}, a spherical Steiner
identity and exact conic kinematics connect a positive coefficient
distribution to intersection probabilities. Appendix E.3, pp.~290--293,
uses Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev concentration and the self-bounding identity
\[
 F(g)=\|\Pi_C(g)\|^2-\delta(C),\qquad
 \|\nabla F(g)\|^2=4\|\Pi_C(g)\|^2.
\]
A Boolean product-space entropy inequality could replace the analytic tool,
but it needs a corresponding difference/influence bound for a SAT-relevant
observable. The elementary clause LP is unsuitable: its all-one-half point
is feasible even for UNSAT formulas (P001 in the survey).

\section{Discrete integral geometry: exact formulas and their limits}
Use signed coordinate permutations as the finite analogue of random
orientations. For $S\subseteq Q_n=\{0,1\}^n$, let $f_j(S)$ count coordinate
$j$-faces all of whose vertices lie in $S$. With
$N_j=2^{n-j}\binom nj$ and uniform signed permutation $g$, an exact identity is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E_g f_j(S\cap gU)=\frac{f_j(S)f_j(U)}{N_j}.
 \label{eq:memo-kinematic}
\end{equation}
It follows from transitivity on $j$-faces. Appendix~\ref{app:cubical}
contains the complete proof, an Euler-characteristic version, and exact
expected face counts under successive random clauses (record P003).

This gives a genuine discrete averaging formula, but two obstructions
prevent a direct substitution into the convex proof:
\begin{itemize}
 \item The proper 3-CNF $(x\lor y\lor z)\land(\neg x\lor\neg y\lor\neg z)$
 has six solutions forming a six-cycle. Its cubical Euler characteristic is
 zero, although the formula is SAT.
 \item $S=\{000,110\}$ and $U=\{000,111\}$ have identical face vectors,
 yet $\mathbb P(S\cap gS\ne\varnothing)=5/12$ and
 $\mathbb P(S\cap gU\ne\varnothing)=1/2$. Face vectors alone do not determine
 intersection probability. They also need not be log-concave: a proper
 6-CNF example in the appendix has face vector $(26,4,1,0,\ldots)$.
\end{itemize}
These are counterexamples to particular transfer steps, not to the existence
of a useful alternative invariant or a typical-instance result for fixed $k$.

Two richer descriptions retain a direct link to nonemptiness. First, a
uniform codimension-$r$ coordinate face $H$ satisfies the discrete
Crofton-type identity
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P(S\cap H\ne\varnothing)
 =\frac{\sum_{|I|=r}|\pi_I S|}{2^r\binom nr},
 \label{eq:memo-crofton}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_I S$ is the set of extendable partial assignments on coordinates
$I$. Second, if $A_d(S)$ counts ordered pairs of solutions at Hamming
distance $d$ and $Z=|S\cap gU|$, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E Z^2=\sum_{d=0}^n
       \frac{A_d(S)A_d(U)}{2^n\binom nd},\qquad
 \mathbb P(Z>0)\ge\frac{(\mathbb E Z)^2}{\mathbb E Z^2}
 \label{eq:memo-overlap}
\end{equation}
when $\mathbb E Z>0$. Both are proved in P003. The first gap is now explicit:
no corresponding simple intersection product rule has been established here
for projection counts, while the second moment needs control of overlap
correlations. Fourier levels provide a
positive representation of the overlap data, familiar from coding theory
\citep[Section 2.1]{navon2007}, but positivity alone gives no sharp SAT bound.

\section{The analytic route to prioritize}
The clause process already supplies the exact probability bridge
$p_{n,k}(m)=\mathbb P(T_n>m)$. It also has a useful geometric state statistic.
If a nonempty solution set has $b$ globally forced coordinates, a fresh
proper random $k$-clause eliminates every solution with probability
\begin{equation}
 h(S)=\frac{\binom bk}{2^k\binom nk}.
 \label{eq:memo-hazard}
\end{equation}
Indeed, all selected coordinates must be constant on $S$, with their signs
opposed to those constants. This is already equation (2.5), p.~5, of
\citet{carenini2026}; it is not a new discovery. That paper's expected
remaining lifetime $r(F)$, defined using a fresh independent continuation
and set to zero for UNSAT $F$, also obeys
$\mathbb E_C r(F\land C)=r(F)-1$ for SAT $F$ (equation (2.4)). These identities
make clause deletion, remaining lifetime and forced coordinates natural
candidates for a discrete replacement of the geometric differential identity.

\paragraph{A precise research target, not a theorem of this note.}
For fixed $k\ge3$, investigate whether the source methods can establish
constants $C_k,b_k>0$, independent of $n$, such that
\begin{equation}
 \log\mathbb E e^{\theta(T_n-\mathbb E T_n)}
 \le \frac{C_k n\theta^2}{2(1-b_k|\theta|)},\qquad
 |\theta|<1/b_k< -\log(1-2^{-k}).
 \label{eq:target-cgf}
\end{equation}
Such a finite-tilt estimate would imply exponential tails and an
$O_k(\sqrt n)$ central clause window. The first unproved step is a suitable
conditional-variance or deletion/influence inequality under the tilted law.
Independent-clause tensorization alone does not establish it: tilting by
$T_n$, or conditioning on SAT, introduces dependence. Clause replacement
can delay the hitting time arbitrarily far along an individual sequence,
so a deterministic unit-Lipschitz argument is unavailable.

The restriction on tilts is essential. P004, proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:analytic}, shows that the mgf of $T_n$ is finite precisely
for $\theta< -\log(1-2^{-k})$. At $n=k=2$, the four-coupon example even gives
$\operatorname{Var}(T_n)=130/9>25/3=\mathbb E T_n$.
Thus the rotation-volume Poisson inequality cannot be copied verbatim.
Moreover, 2-SAT has an $n^{2/3}$ clause window \citep[Theorem 1.1]{bbckw2001},
so it is a useful control rather than evidence for an all-$k$ square-root law.
Recent retained manuscripts already claim linear variance for $k\ge3$
\citep{oaVariance,oaVariance3}. The proposal is to seek a structural geometric
argument or stronger tails, not to claim that the variance order is new.
Those manuscripts' full proofs have not been audited here.

\paragraph{A secondary route and its limitation.}
Let $H_F(x)$ count violated clauses and set
$Z_\beta(F)=\sum_x e^{-\beta H_F(x)}$, for $\beta>0$. Replacing one clause
changes $\log Z_\beta$ by at most $\beta$ and $\min_xH_F(x)$ by at most one,
so product-space concentration applies to these observables. However,
\[
 -\beta\min_xH_F(x)\le\log Z_\beta(F)
 \le n\log2-\beta\min_xH_F(x).
\]
The crude entropy error requires $\beta$ of order $n$ to resolve a unit
ground-state gap, while the elementary concentration scale grows with
$\beta$. Concentration of the energy therefore does not by itself control
its atom at zero. A useful discrete Poincare or entropy inequality for the
assignment measure needs additional structure, especially near the transition.

\section{What phase diagrams should measure}
The ALMT experiments use repeated trials to estimate success probabilities;
Figures 1 and 5 and Appendix A.1--A.3 describe both the images and their
sampling \citep{almt2014}. The $d=100$ compressed-sensing design has
$100\times100\times50=500{,}000$ convex solves. A separate low-rank panel
required nearly a month on a desktop. These are methodological examples,
not SAT runtime estimates.

For fixed $k$, put $n$ on the horizontal axis and $m/n$ on the vertical axis.
Colour indicates the SAT probability $p_{n,k}(m)$. Overlay estimated 5\%,
50\% and 95\% contours, and provide a separate unknown/timeout panel.
Plot the clause-count window and its density-normalized version. Solver
success within a time limit is a different observable. A fitted curve is
not a geometric theorem predicting the k-SAT boundary.

\paragraph{Direct grid.}
A proposed first 3-SAT study uses about eight sizes, 31 densities and
100 independent formulas per cell: $24{,}800$ runs. Start in the
$50$--$200$ range, and admit $250$ only after a longer calibration. A broad
coarse density range such as $3.5$--$5.0$ can locate the transition before
refinement. Use fresh samples for the final refined grid, and retain the
actual integer $m$. At probability one half, 100 trials give an approximate
95\% pointwise uncertainty of $\pm0.10$; 400 give about $\pm0.05$.
These are not simultaneous confidence bands over the image. Add a scalable
2-SAT control; pilot 4-SAT separately at smaller sizes.

\paragraph{Independent coupled paths.}
An alternative samples independent clause sequences for each $n$ and
estimates their $T_n$. Every completed path supplies the entire survival
curve, so prefixes should not be counted as independent repetitions. A
certified SAT prefix at $L$ and UNSAT prefix at $U$ give $T_n\in(L,U]$;
bisection needs at most $\lceil\log_2(U-L)\rceil$ additional decisive queries.
Unknown queries do not determine a side of the bracket. Incremental solving
can reuse work; bisection requires fresh solvers or correct activation
assumptions, whose overhead must be measured \citep{pysatAPI}.

For $R$ fully classified independent paths at one $n$, the one-sample
DKW bound gives a uniform-in-$m$ 95\% absolute error
$\sqrt{\log(40)/(2R)}$; $R=738$ suffices for $0.05$
\citep[Section 1, equation (1)]{wei2011}. More rows require a simultaneous
adjustment. With $S$ verified SAT and $U$ verified UNSAT outcomes among $R$
fixed-cell instances, censoring alone gives $[S/R,1-U/R]$ for the sample's
SAT fraction; population sampling uncertainty is additional. Preserve
timeouts, verify assignments, and include UNSAT proof-checking cost in a
final certified study.

\section{Computational complexity and the measured pilot}
Generating a formula and checking a witness cost $O(km)$; packed 32-bit
literal indices alone need about $4km$ bytes. Complete 2-SAT decision is
$O(n+m)$. For $k\ge3$, exhaustive decision costs $O(km2^n)$ and practical
complete solvers have no applicable polynomial worst-case guarantee.
The cost near the threshold depends strongly on $k$, $n$, density and whether
the answer is SAT or UNSAT. Large-$k$ near-threshold inputs already have
$m$ of order $2^k n$. Enumerating full solution/face profiles is substantially
more expensive than deciding SAT; reserve it for a separate small-$n$
exploration, initially around $n=12$--$18$, subject to a separate pilot.

\paragraph{E001: a bounded runtime calibration.}
The delegated agent ran 60 proper random 3-CNF instances on an Apple M3,
using CaDiCaL 1.9.5 through PySAT 1.9.dev15. Each size had five instances
at each of densities $4.2$ and $4.3$. The per-process cutoff was 0.75 seconds,
including Python startup, input parsing and initialization. The total was
30.89 seconds elapsed and 29.35 child CPU seconds. Inputs, seeds, versions,
script hashes, outputs and witnesses are retained in E001.

\midrule
100 & 6 & 4 & 0 & 0.074\\

All 21 SAT witnesses were checked; the nine UNSAT reports have no checked
proofs. All 30 timeouts remain unknown. Means include capped runs, so they
are not estimates of uncensored completion times. This pilot supports a
modest initial range; it does not identify a largest feasible $n$, estimate
a transition curve, or measure proof-checking overhead. Five instances per
cell are inadequate for those purposes.

\paragraph{Conditional budgets.}
For about 25,000 runs with mean total processing time $t$ seconds, the work
is $25{,}000t/3600$ core-hours. Include generation, solving, cutoff handling
and certification in $t$ when measuring the target system.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
Mean $t$ & Core-hours & Ideal elapsed time, 64 CPU workers\\
\midrule
0.1 s & 0.69 & 39 s\\
1 s & 6.94 & 6.5 min\\
10 s & 69.4 & 1.09 h\\
60 s & 416.7 & 6.51 h\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts extrapolated from the short
pilot. Parallel overhead, stragglers and queue time increase elapsed time.
Batch small instances and distribute independent single-core solves before
trying to accelerate each individual hard formula with many cores.

\paragraph{CPU cluster first; GPU experiments later.}
The saved SCRTP hardware documentation lists 168-core AMD Genoa Blythe
nodes with 768 GB RAM and GPU nodes with three L40 48 GB devices
\citep{scrtpHardware}. This makes independent CPU batches a natural design.
These are snapshot facts, not a live resource allocation or benchmark;
site limits and current availability must be checked when preparing jobs.
GPU inprocessing and batched local search have published evidence
\citep{osama2024,cen2025}, but do not establish an end-to-end advantage for
complete near-threshold random-CNF classification. A failed GPU search
does not prove UNSAT. The first diagram should use CPU solving and the
chosen certificate policy; GPU witness-search success can be studied in a
separate panel. No SLURM scripts or cluster jobs were created in this assessment.

\section{Next discriminating steps and status}
For mathematics, prioritize the finite-tilt clause-process route, with
\eqref{eq:memo-hazard} as its exact geometric bridge. In parallel, compare
projection and overlap statistics at exhaustively accessible sizes. The
critical task is an invariant with both a valid nonemptiness relation and
a strong, model-specific concentration inequality. P003 excludes several
naive face-vector substitutions; P004 fixes the possible tilt domain.
Neither rules out a more suitable discrete geometry.

For computation, the feasibility answer is yes for modest CPU diagrams.
The next practical step is a longer pilot on the intended SCRTP node,
including proof generation/checking, before choosing the upper $n$ range,
repetition count and batch sizes. The supplied documentation can then guide
SLURM scripts. No GPU purchase or large allocation is needed to establish
the first useful diagram.

P003 and P004 are elementary results with separate internal reviews by
agents other than their authors, at the hashes recorded in their review
files. This is not external refereeing or formal verification. The transfer
programme is A001 and remains proposed; E001 is a completed, limited runtime
calibration. No new threshold theorem or validated scaling law is claimed.
